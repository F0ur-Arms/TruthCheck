Pizza, an irresistible combination of a crisp crust,
gooey cheese, and a tantalizing medley of toppings, has secured a special place in the hearts and palates of people worldwide.
Beyond its delectable composition, pizza often stands as a nostalgic cornerstone in many lives, evoking memories of shared moments, casual gatherings, and carefree indulgence. From its origins in the vibrant kitchens of Italy to now being a culinary delight worldwide, pizza has earned its status as an emblem of pleasure.
But, where did it all start? Where did the idea of throwing tomato and cheese on a dough come from? If you guessed the answer to the question above as Italy… Well, you are only partly right.
Although most people are of the opinion that Italy is the birthplace of Pizza, historians believe that Greeks, Romans and Egyptians were already making dishes similar to pizza. According to records, Persians baked flatbreads covered in cheese and varied toppings just like artisanal pizza shops bake today.
But the pizza, as we know it today, was first made in 18th century Naples. During the rule of the Bourbon kings, Naples burgeoned into one of Europe's largest and rapidly expanding cities. Fueled by maritime trade and a constant influx of rural inhabitants, its population surged from 200,000 in 1700 to 399,000 by 1748.
Among the most impoverished of the lot was the Lazzaroni, named so due to their tattered appearance akin to Lazarus. This group subsisted on meager earnings from jobs like those of porters, messengers, and casual laborers. They required sustenance that was both affordable and convenient. And so happened the birth of pizzas – a solution to this need.
Rather than being retailed in stores, these pizzas were sold by street hawkers and the vendors would slice the pizzas to suit each customer's budget and appetite.
In Le Corricolo (1843), Alexandre Dumas aptly observed, a two liard slice could serve as a hearty breakfast, while two sous could secure a pizza sizable enough to feed an entire family.
Unfortunately, in 18th-century Naples, pizza was looked down upon by the upper classes. Closely linked with the dire destitution of the lazzaroni, these culinary creations were frequently scorned as 'repugnant,' particularly by foreign visitors.
So, how did Pizza become famous and widely liked, just as it is today?
During a trip to Naples in 1889, King Umberto I and Queen
Margherita grew tired of the complex French meals they were having. They asked a local pizza maker, Raffaele Esposito, to make them some local dishes. He made three kinds of pizza: one with lard, cheese, and basil; another with a type of small fish; and a third with tomatoes, mozzarella cheese, and basil. The queen liked the third one the most and they named it "pizza margherita" after the queen.
How is traditional Italian pizza made?
For a fresh Italian pizza, the outer layer, or the crust, is really crucial. It's usually thin and fluffy and holds the special taste and amazing texture that you only get in Italian pizza. Chefs think it's really important to use just the right amount of fresh yeast and a type of flour called "00" flour to make the perfect crust. They stretch the crust by hand and cook it at super high temperatures in a wood-fired oven to make it turn out really great.
Post the making of the perfect crust, the sauce is really important and is usually made from special San Marzano tomatoes. They then mix these tomatoes with the right amount of herbs before putting them atop the pizza.
Occasionally, Italian pizzas don't even have a sauce. Instead, they put a little bit of olive oil on it.
The toppings on an Italian pizza are an even better business.
While pizzas as we see them host mini pepperoni or chicken shreds, Italian pizzas have eggplant, artichokes, pumpkin, and even truffles as toppings.
Pizza Capricciosa, pizza Pugliese, pizza Veronese, and
Quattro Formaggi are some of the authentic pizzas being baked in Italian ovens.
Traditional pizza has now transformed into an amalgamation of different cultures. The dough, the sauce, and the toppings have all received their fair share of culture shocks. From deep dish crusts to makhani sauces, the way pizza is made has transformed over the years and around the world. Here we list down some popular pizzas from across the world:
1. Neapolitan Pizza : The classic Italian pizza, with a thin crust, simple tomato sauce or drizzled olive oil, fresh mozzarella cheese, and fresh basil. It's often cooked in a wood-fired oven for a short time.
2. New York-Style Pizza : Popular for its large, foldable slices and cheap prices, New York-style pizza has a hand-tossed, thin crust. They are typically topped with tomato sauce and mozzarella cheese and can be loaded with various toppings, according to choice.
3. Chicago Deep-Dish Pizza: A thick, deep crust is the hallmark of Chicago Pizza. It's layered with cheese, toppings, and tomato sauce, often in reverse order, and resembles a casserole dish cooked to crisp perfection.
4. Sicilian Pizza: Originating from Sicily but popular in the US as well, this pizza is rectangular with a thicker, fluffy crust. It's often topped with a chunky tomato sauce and various toppings.
5. Calzone: A folded pizza, with its edges, closed shut, where the dough is filled with cheese, sauce, and other pizza ingredients before being baked.
6. Turkish Pide: Resembling a boat, Turkish pide features a thin crust filled with various toppings like minced meat, cheese, vegetables, and eggs and twisted on the opposite sides.
7. Indian Pizza: In India, you can find pizzas made with Indian sauces and appropriately spiced toppings. They're topped with various Indian-inspired ingredients like paneer, chicken tikka, makhani gravy, and varied spices.
Thus, pizza has played a very flexible role and adjusted according to the countries it went to. While it is cooked with bacon grease in one country, it is only drizzled with a bit of olive oil in the other. Similarly, while the tomato sauce is left alone without many spices, makhani sauces as a base has emerged in other places. And this flexibility is what has made pizza a household favorite.
