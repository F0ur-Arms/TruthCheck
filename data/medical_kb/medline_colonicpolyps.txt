Also called: Colon polyps, Colorectal polyps
What are colonic polyps?
Colonic polyps are growths on the inner lining of your colon or rectum. The rectum is the last part of your large intestine. Polyps are common in adults, especially as you get older. You can have one or more polyps.
There are different types of polyps. Most are benign (noncancerous), but some are precancerous and may turn into cancer over time. Finding and removing polyps can help prevent colorectal cancer. During a procedure called a colonoscopy, your health care provider can examine your colon and remove any polyps.
Who is more likely to develop colonic polyps?
Anyone can get polyps, but they are more common in men and older adults. There are certain factors that may raise your risk of developing colonic polyps:
Being age 45 or older
Having had polyps before
Having a family history of polyps or colorectal cancer
Having inflammatory bowel disease, such as ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease
Having obesity
Smoking
What causes colonic polyps?
The cause of colonic polyps is unknown. It's believed that age, family history, and lifestyle may all play a role.
What are the symptoms of colonic polyps?
Most colonic polyps do not cause symptoms. If you do have symptoms, they may include:
Bleeding from your rectum. You might see blood on your underwear or on toilet paper after a bowel movement (poop).
Blood in your stool (poop). Blood may appear as red streaks or make stool look black.
Fatigue. Bleeding from polyps can cause anemia, which means you don't have enough red blood cells. Anemia can make you feel tired.
Changes in your bowel habits. This could include constipation or diarrhea that lasts more than a week.
Many other health problems can also cause these symptoms. But if you notice bleeding from your rectum or blood in your stool, contact your provider right away.
How are colonic polyps diagnosed?
Providers use tests and procedures to find colonic polyps. Screening tests look for polyps or colorectal cancer in people who do not have any symptoms. These tests include:
Colonoscopy, which uses a thin tube with a light and tiny camera to look inside your rectum and colon.
Flexible sigmoidoscopy, which uses a thin tube with a light and tiny camera to check your rectum and the lower part of your colon (sigmoid colon).
Virtual colonoscopy, which uses x-rays and a computer to create images of your colon. If the test finds a possible polyp, you will usually need a colonoscopy.
Stool tests, which look for blood or other signs of colorectal cancer. An abnormal result may require you to have a follow-up colonoscopy.
Most adults should start colorectal cancer screening at age 45. If you are more likely to get polyps or colorectal cancer, your provider may suggest starting sooner or testing more often.
What are the treatments for colonic polyps?
Most polyps can be removed during a colonoscopy or flexible sigmoidoscopy. The polyps are then tested for cancer. Rarely, you may need surgery to remove a large polyp or part of your colon if it can't be removed during one of these procedures.
Can colonic polyps be prevented?
There is no way to prevent colonic polyps, but healthy lifestyle habits may help lower your risk:
Eating a healthy diet that includes fruits, vegetables, and other foods high in fiber
Limiting highly processed and fried foods
Getting regular exercise
Maintaining a healthy weight
Quitting smoking (or not starting)
Getting screened is one of the best ways to find colonic polyps, so they can be removed before they become cancerous.
