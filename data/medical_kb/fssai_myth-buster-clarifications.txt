Clarification on ice cream and frozen dessert
Ice cream and frozen dessert are two standardized products under FSS (FPS and FA) Regulation 2011 and have to follow FSS (Labelling) Regulation along with specific labelling requirements mentioned in the standards itself. By virtue of ingredients including vegetable oil allowed to be used for preparation of frozen desserts as specified in the specific standards, the product is safe for human consumption, if it complies with the requirements of FSSR.
Clarification on packaged salt being adulterated with detergent
A video surfaced on social media channels, claiming adulteration of salt with detergent. Generally, a maximum of 35 grams of pure salt can be dissolved in 100 ml of water (known as a saturated solution). If more salt is added to this water, it will not get dissolved. In the video, a large amount of salt is being mixed in a small quantity of water, leading to the formation of lumps and foam and being wrongly perceived as adulteration with detergent. Also, a small amount of insoluble particles, silica, phosphates, sulphates, permitted food additives, and in case of Double Fortified Salt (DFS), additionally Encapsulated Ferrous Fumarate (EFF) particles may be visible. These are permissible as per FSS (Food Product Standards and Food Additives) Regulation, 2011 and are not considered adulterants.
Clarification on standards for chocolate, allowing insects
According to FSSAI's standards, any chocolate should be free from insects and other contaminants. Any rumour stating that FSSAI has permitted insects in the chocolate manufacturing process is false.
Clarification regarding consumption of poultry products and corona virus (2020)
Poultry has not been found to be involved in transmission of 2019-nCoV to humans so far in any report globally. Similarly, outbreaks of Corona Virus in the past (SARS 2002-03, MERS 2012-13) or Corona-associated common cold had no involvement of poultry or poultry products world over. Thus, with present knowledge of affairs of 2019-nCoV, consumption of poultry and poultry products may be considered safe. General principles of hygiene, however, may be followed as per suggestions of WHO/OIE.
Clarification regarding ferrocyanides in salt
Ferrocyanides are approved anti-caking agents for common salt including edible common salt, iron fortified salt and iodized salt as per FSSAI Regulations. In India it is allowed at a level of 10 mg/kg compared to EU regulations where it is allowed 20 mg/kg of salt.
Clarification about plastic eggs
There is no technology or chemicals available to complete the manufacturing of whole eggs. Also, it is not an economically viable process since normal eggs are available in abundance and at an affordable price. A thick eggshell membrane indicates the freshness of the eggs. The reason why eggs differ from each other is because of a difference in quality of feed, breed of bird, ageing of eggs and the handling of eggs.
Clarification regarding melamine in infant milk
Melamine has no known food uses. It must be noted that the use of melamine either as an ingredient or as an additive is not permitted in any food by FSSAI, although maximum limits for presence of melamine in food, including milk, have been prescribed to address the incidental presence of melamine as a contaminant. Setting up of such limits certainly does not mean that FSSAI allows addition of melamine to foods even within the specified maximum limit.
Clarification regarding presence of plastic in wheat flour
Wheat flour naturally contains two proteins – Glutenin (for elasticity) and Gliadin (for raising of dough). In the presence of water, these two proteins bond together to create an elastic network of protein called gluten strings. Gluten is the rubbery mass that is left when wheat flour dough is washed with water, and this is maliciously referred to as "plastic".
Clarification regarding presence of plastic rice in market
It is a natural phenomenon of rice to burn since it is a complex carbohydrate, and since rice is 80% starch, it has cohesive and adhesive properties. When the rice is cooked and transformed into a ball, air gets entrapped and it becomes bouncy like a ball. Thus, it should be ruled out that the rice contains plastic.
Clarification regarding presence of plastic in snacks
Snacks such as namkeens and potato chips are mainly made of cereals, spices and oil, and carbohydrates and fats have a natural characteristic of burning when exposed to fire. Thus, it should be ruled out that they contain plastic.
