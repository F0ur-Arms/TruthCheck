TITLE: Why one should add protein-rich shrimps to their daily diet
URL: https://recipes.timesofindia.com/articles/food-facts/why-one-should-add-protein-rich-shrimps-to-their-daily-diet/articleshow/103960925.cms
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

When we think of seafood, a range of culinary delights like succulent salmon, tender tuna, delicious crabs, and juicy shrimp often come to mind. They not only tantalize our taste buds but also hold high nutritional value. Among these, shrimp hold unique value in some cultural cuisines around the world. Its mild, salty flavor with a trace of natural sweetness makes it a good choice not just as a flavourful delicacy but as a highly nutritious meal.
Most athletes and fitness enthusiasts always seek ways to fuel their bodies with the right nutrients. And shrimp can be the perfect addition to their meals. Low in calories, carbs, and fats, but high in protein, crucial for muscle recovery and growth – it's an ideal option for those watching their macros or following low-carb, low-calorie diets.
Along with protein, these tiny and delicious crustaceans contain omega-3 fatty acids, known for their heart-healthy properties. It is also rich in selenium, a potent antioxidant that supports overall health, and vitamin B12, vital for nerve function and the production of red blood cells. Including shrimp in the diet also helps gain iodine, an essential mineral for thyroid and brain health.
Notably, shrimp also provides a modest but meaningful amount of vitamins A and C, as well as calcium and iron, making it a well-rounded nutritional package. Around an 84-gram serving of shrimp provides 4% of an individual's daily recommended intake (DV) for vitamins A and C, along with 6% and 10% DV for calcium and iron, respectively.
Meeting protein requirements sustainably Today’s pressing challenge lies in the need to significantly boost food production by 60% to feed the world population, which is projected to reach 9.3 billion by 2050. This surge in demand, combined with climate change and its impact on food production systems, is creating pressure on food security and nutritional demand.
India ranks second in aquaculture production. Today, every second fish on our plate is farmed (in contrast to the marine capture from oceans). The country exported around 7 lac tons of shrimp in FY 22–23 alone. Recent tech-enabled innovations in aquaculture have the potential to further improve the production and export trajectory. In fact, India is well poised to be one of the leading contributors to fulfilling the global demand for nutritious, protein-rich food while also fulfilling the protein demand in the domestic market.
ConclusionIn an era where individuals are looking for nutritious, healthy, and sustainable food sources, shrimp is an appealing choice. Its impressive protein content and a rich array of essential nutrients make it a perfect addition to a balanced diet, especially for anyone prioritizing their health and fitness.
Moreover, sustainably produced shrimp address concerns about overfishing and environmental impact, ensuring that future generations can continue to enjoy this delectable seafood. So, the next time you savor the succulent taste of shrimp, you can relish its deliciousness and the nutritional benefits it brings to your plate without feeling guilty.
